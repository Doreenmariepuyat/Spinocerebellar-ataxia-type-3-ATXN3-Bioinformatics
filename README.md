## PART C. Exons, Introns, and Transcripts
a. Number of exons you can identify in your selected transcript

The selected transcript is NM_004993.6 (ATXN3), the MANE Select transcript. It contains 11 exons.

b. Whether multiple transcripts/isoforms are visible

Yes. Multiple ATXN3 transcript models are visible in the GENCODE and NCBI RefSeq tracks. These transcript models show differences in transcript structure and indicate that the ATXN3 genomic region can produce multiple transcript isoforms.

c. In your own words, explain the difference between an exon and an intron

Exons are parts of a gene that remain in the mature RNA after RNA splicing. They can contain coding information used to produce a protein. Introns are intervening sequences that are removed from the RNA during splicing.

d. Describe whether the introns generally appear longer or shorter than the exons in your gene

The introns generally appear longer than the individual exons in the ATXN3 gene. In the UCSC Genome Browser, the exon blocks are relatively small compared with the larger gaps between them that represent intronic regions.

c. Were some regions more conserved than others?

d. Did conserved regions correspond mainly to exons, introns, both, or another region?

The conserved regions could be observed in both exonic and intronic regions. The degree of conservation varied across the ATXN3 genomic region.

e. In 2–3 sentences, explain why strong conservation can suggest biological importance.

Strong conservation means that a DNA region has remained similar across different species over evolutionary time. This can suggest that the region is biologically important because changes in highly conserved regions may be less tolerated and could affect normal gene function. However, conservation alone does not prove that a particular variant is harmful.

PART F. Locating the Variant in UCSC
a. Where is the variant located relative to your gene?

The selected variant is found within the ATXN3 gene on chromosome 14. Its GRCh38 position is chr14:92,071,010.

b. Is it in an exon, intron, UTR, splice region, or another region?

The variant is located in an exon of the ATXN3 gene.

c. Is it likely in a coding or non-coding region based on the displayed annotations?

It is in the coding region because the position is within the part of the transcript that codes for the ATXN3 protein. The UCSC view places it around Glycine 306 (G306).

d. Based on its location and ClinVar information, briefly explain how the variant might affect the gene or gene product.

The change replaces one nucleotide in the ATXN3 coding sequence. This changes the amino acid from glycine to arginine, written as p.Gly306Arg. Since this is a missense change, it can potentially affect the protein, although ClinVar reports this particular variant as Benign for the ATXN3-related disorder condition.

e. What additional evidence would be needed before concluding that the variant causes disease?

More evidence would be needed, such as laboratory experiments, information from people who carry the variant, population data, and other genetic studies. These types of evidence can help determine whether the variant actually has a harmful effect.
